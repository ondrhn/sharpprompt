# Tidying long prompts (11 October 2026)

In two days of the author's use (9 October 22:40 to 11 October 22:00) 15 of 40 rough prompts timed out at 5 seconds, median 32 words; every rewrite that came back was 31 words or shorter. Read side by side, the long drafts were not vague. They repeated themselves, jumped between points and left the order of the work implicit. So from 60 words a draft skips the classifier and gets a tidy instruction instead of the rewrite rules (`TIDY` in `hooks/rewrite.ts`), with the draft's own length as the word cap.

## Six real drafts

The six timed-out drafts of 60 words or more, Turkish, tidied by `claude -p` on Opus 5.5 with the fork prompt and no conversation. The drafts stay outside the repository.

| words | API time | output tokens | words out | check |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 60 | 2.5 s | 239 | 52 | kept all |
| 70 | 7.1 s | 745 | 70 | kept all |
| 90 | 10.9 s | 692 | 75 | kept all |
| 107 | 7.9 s | 908 | 96 | kept all |
| 113 | 6.7 s | 647 | 98 | kept all |
| 253 | 21.2 s | 2,625 | 243 | lost: the `<pasted_content>` tags |

Five of six came back as numbered requests in working order, repeats merged, nothing added that the draft did not say. The sixth was mostly a pasted answer from another assistant; the tidy dropped the tags around it, which would have made the pasted text read as the user's own. The check caught it, and the instruction now says to keep such a block as it is.

Output tokens run 3 to 10 times the words written, so most of the output is thinking, and time follows it more than the draft's length. Three of the six would have timed out under the limit set earlier the same day (5 s + 50 ms a word past 20, at most 15 s), so it is now 5 s + 100 ms a word past 20, at most 25 s. These runs had no conversation behind them; a fork reads the whole session from the cache, which may add to the time.

## The check

`anchors()` takes from the draft every code span, quoted text, URL, path, file name and number (list markers aside); a rewrite missing any of them is turned down as `lost` and the prompt goes out as typed. On the 28 rewrites in the author's usage log it flagged none.
