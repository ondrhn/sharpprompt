# Benchmark, Fable 5.1: no measurable difference

On Fable 5.1 with this 30-case corpus, no metric showed a difference; all intervals include zero.

Run on 8 to 9 October 2026 with Claude Code 2.1.295, model `claude-fable-5-1`, one repeat. Raw data: [runs/2026-10-08-claude-fable-5-1.jsonl](../bench/runs/2026-10-08-claude-fable-5-1.jsonl) and its manifest. Corpus, fixture, checks and rewrites: [docs/bench](../bench).

## Paired result

26 cases (the 4 where the rewrite model answered KEEP send the same text in both arms and are left out). Difference is rewritten minus raw, per case; the interval is a bootstrap of the median difference, 1,000 resamples, fixed seed.

| metric | raw median | rewritten median | median paired difference | 95% interval |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| turns | 7.5 | 9.5 | +0.5 | 0 to +2 |
| tool calls | 6.5 | 8.5 | +0.5 | 0 to +2 |
| output tokens | 1,458 | 1,773 | +31.5 | -219 to +530 |
| seconds | 28.4 | 26.2 | 0 | -6.5 to +5.3 |

- Final message ended on a question: raw 0 of 26, rewritten 0 of 26.
- Question words in the last lines ("which", "do you mean", "want me to" and similar): raw 6, rewritten 8. Most of these are offers of a next step ("want me to add a test?"), not requests for clarification.
- Checks: raw 12 of 12, rewritten 12 of 12 (5 fix, 5 build, 2 refactor cases with a check). Both arms finished every checked task, so the checks did not separate them.

## Blind judge

Added after the run. Sonnet 5.5 as judge, for each of the 26 paired cases: the user's request with its context, the case's `expect` sentence, and the two final answers in a random order with the arms hidden. It sees the final messages only, not the files changed. Verdicts: [runs/2026-10-08-claude-fable-5-1.judge.jsonl](../bench/runs/2026-10-08-claude-fable-5-1.judge.jsonl).

- Pairwise: rewritten 16, raw 9, tie 1; sign test p = 0.23.
- Score from 1 to 5 for each answer on its own: raw mean 3.92, rewritten mean 3.88, mean paired difference -0.04 (n=26).
- The judge's two answers agree with each other in direction in 9 cases, disagree in 7, and are neutral in 10. In ref-dates, for one, it preferred the rewritten arm's answer ("no changes were needed") in the pairwise question and scored the same answer 2 of 5 on its own.

Where it picked the rewritten arm it often pointed at things the rewrite had asked for, such as line numbers in a review ("lists all the expected problems ... with specific line numbers"). Read together with the numbers above: no difference shown.

## Method

Each case ran twice on a fresh copy of the fixture, once with the prompt as typed and once with the rewrite, in a random order recorded per case. A case's earlier messages went first as their own turn, the same text for both arms, and only the last turn was measured. Runs used `claude -p` with `--setting-sources ''`, `--permission-mode acceptEdits` and `--allowedTools 'Bash(python3 *)'`. Details and the command: [docs/bench/README.md](../bench/README.md).

Cost, as the API would bill it (the runs used a Max plan): 30.43 USD for the measured turns and 5.26 USD for the context turns, 35.68 USD in all. Time: 2,011 seconds.

## Limits

- We wrote the corpus. It may lean either way; all 30 cases are in the repository.
- One repeat. The noise above is larger than most differences we could hope to see.
- The rewrites come from the plugin's fallback template with the context pasted in, not from a fork of a live conversation, and no classifier picked which prompts to rewrite.
- Fable 5.1 handled these rough prompts well on its own: it never asked a clarifying question in either arm, so there was little for a rewrite to remove.
- Two rewrites widened the task (ref-dates: "across the codebase"; build-biggest: "for each month"). They ran as generated.
- Both arms loaded the account's `~/.claude/CLAUDE.md`.
